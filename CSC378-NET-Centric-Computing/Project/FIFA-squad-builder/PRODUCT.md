# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Students and football-squad builders use this as a local web app for building EA FC-style squads. They are usually in a task flow: pick a formation, add or swap players, compare ratings, watch budget, and identify weak positions without leaving the squad screen.

Admins use the same app to import players, maintain formation data, manage users, and attach locally cached card artwork.

## Product Purpose

FIFA Squad Builder helps users create, inspect, and tune football squads with real player data, formation-aware pitch placement, wage and value visibility, chemistry, and weak-position recommendations. Success means the squad screen feels like a working squad-builder tool rather than a generic CRUD app.

## Brand Personality

Tactical, grounded, personal. The interface should feel like a custom-built squad room: focused on the pitch, direct in its controls, and confident enough to avoid unnecessary decoration.

The tone is compact and practical. Labels should help users act quickly, not explain the product like a landing page.

## Anti-references

Do not make it look like the default Bootstrap starter template.

Do not copy FUTWIZ, FUTBIN, EA, or any other third-party builder branding into the pitch or app chrome. Use the useful category pattern, but keep FIFA Squad Builder visually independent.

Do not use generic AI-looking decoration: oversized marketing cards, purple-blue gradients, floating blobs, vague hero copy, or feature descriptions that explain obvious UI.

Do not let the pitch become cluttered with banners, slogans, watermarks, or decorative text. The pitch is the workspace.

## Design Principles

1. Put the pitch first. The squad screen is the product, and every visual decision should make players, slots, and formation easier to scan.
2. Use real football artifacts. Complete card artwork, ratings, positions, budgets, and weak spots should carry the experience more than decorative CSS.
3. Keep controls deliberate. Formation switching, saving, player search, and admin tools should look stable, predictable, and hard to misread.
4. Make density calm. The app can show many numbers, but spacing, contrast, and grouping must keep the interface readable.
5. Own the identity. The project can be inspired by squad-builder conventions without importing another site's labels, watermarks, or brand language.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for text and controls. Do not encode important state only through color; pair color with text such as rating, budget state, or position labels. Keep keyboard focus visible on links, buttons, form fields, cards, and modal controls. Avoid decorative motion; transitions should be short and tied to hover, focus, or selection state.
